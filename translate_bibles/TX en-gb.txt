## (1) General principles (en-GB)

### Target positioning & reader promise
- **Genre register:** *literary crime / high-end thriller* with satirical contemporary-media edge. The book moves between noir-ish observation, procedural coolness, and internet-chaos immediacy.
- **Setting:** unmistakably Finland (Helsinki, Vuosaari harbour, Kallio flats, Pasila police station, Musiikkitalo, Assembly, Finnish retail chains). **Do not relocate** or “domesticate” into UK equivalents.
- **Reader experience:** culturally legible to a UK general reader *without smoothing away Finnishness*. The “foreignness” is part of the texture; comprehension is maintained through context and minimal glosses.

### Narrative voice: tone, cadence, understatement
- **Narration** often has:
  - precise visual/aural detail (fog, horns, concrete, tram screech),
  - architectural/engineering metaphors (Arkkitehti; “silence as mortar”),
  - essayistic mini-lectures (history segments; institutional critique),
  - sudden hard cuts into chat/feeds/headlines.
- In en-GB, aim for **controlled lyricism**—clean, exact sentences with occasional poetic lift. Avoid melodrama: keep **understatement** where Finnish original is laconic.
- Preserve the **moral coldness** of platform logic: tragedy + sponsor overlay sits side by side; do not editorialise beyond what the text already does.

### Dialogue: rhythm and social texture
- Finnish dialogue in the text is frequently **short, clipped, functional**. UK English should keep:
  - **brevity** (don’t over-elaborate),
  - **subtext** (let awkwardness sit),
  - **timing** (pauses and broken lines matter).
- **Miksu**: salesy, “bro” energy. Keep contemporary but not aggressively gen‑Z; avoid US hype-speak overload. Use UK-friendly equivalents sparingly (“mate”, “look”, “honestly”) while keeping his *internet pitch cadence*.
- **Virtanen**: procedural, weary, dry. Keep his professional economy; he should not sound like a US cop. Avoid “Miranda” phrasing; keep it generic (“You have the right…”), as per the novel’s already-generalised procedural language.
- **Arkkitehti**: controlled, aestheticised, quasi-liturgical. Keep his diction **formal, crisp, slightly archaic-philosophical**, with a preference for structure/measure/silence.

### Humour & irony (British dryness)
- Much humour is **deadpan, cynical, observational**, often meta-media satire (clickbait, algorithms, moral panic).
- Translate jokes for **effect** using UK-style dry humour and understatement rather than punchline-heavy rewriting.
- Avoid replacing Finnish references with UK analogues. The comedy is often precisely that it is **Finland doing global internet theatre**.

### Maintaining crime tension (procedural + spectacle)
- Preserve the tension created by:
  - delayed information,
  - institutional boundaries (“we can’t comment”),
  - evidence handling (clips, VODs, hashes),
  - public spectacle vs investigative reality.
- Keep “administrative chill” as part of suspense: the horror is not only murder, but **process**.

### Twitch chat / chorus / system voice: distinct layers
The novel uses multiple “meta-voices”:
1. **Twitch chat**: fast, memetic, often bilingual, emojis, abbreviations.
2. **News headlines & media snippets**: different outlets with different moral registers.
3. **Chorus/chorus_AI/system**: tragic chorus + machine logic + occult glitch.
**Principle:** preserve each layer’s typographic identity and register boundaries.
- Keep handles in brackets, fast line breaks, caps, emoji where present.
- Keep technical strings (SHA-256, LUFS, bitrate, GO LIVE) as-is unless standard UK spelling applies.
- For “chorus” passages, allow slightly elevated diction, but avoid faux-Shakespeare; it should feel **modern-tragic**, not pastiche.

### Swearing & colloquial language (en-GB)
- Avoid US profanity patterns (“ass”, “dude”, “freaking” as default).
- Map Finnish swear intensity to UK equivalents:
  - “vittu” often → “fuck / fucking” (context-dependent).
  - “helvetti” → “hell” / “bloody hell” / “what the hell”.
  - “perkele” is culturally specific: usually translate by effect (“for fuck’s sake”), but **optionally** keep *perkele* once or twice as Finnish flavour if the scene benefits—do so consistently and without turning it into a catchphrase.
- Avoid heavy UK regional dialect writing (no “innit” everywhere). Keep it **broadly standard UK contemporary**, with occasional “mate” where it fits character voice.

### Anglicisms & technical language
- Twitch/gaming vocabulary can remain **native English** (stream, chat, clip, mod, ban, VOD, overlay, slow mode).
- Maintain controlled technical precision (e.g., LUFS, bitrate, x264 10-bit). Do not “explain tech” unless the Finnish text already implies explanation; the voice often *uses* tech as atmosphere and authority.
- Keep e-sports names and map names exactly: Counter-Strike: Global Offensive, DE_DUST, etc.

### When to explain vs leave as-is
**Footnotes discouraged** (thriller pacing + multi-voice format).
Use:
- **Keep original + explain once** for recurring Finnish-specific institutions, places, retail chains, media outlets.
- **Brief in-sentence gloss** when needed to avoid confusion at first mention.
- **Unobtrusive appositive** to anchor meaning without “tour-guide” tone.
Leave unexplained:
- minor Finnish flavour items that are self-evident via context (tram screech, fog, “pipo” once it’s established as beanie).
- proper names and street names (Tryvikintie, Bulevardi) without translating into “Boulevard”.

### C. 10 concrete rules for the translator (en-GB)
1. **Keep Finland on the page:** do not swap Finnish institutions/brands for UK equivalents. No “Tesco”, no “the Met”, no “the Guardian”.
2. **Understatement over melodrama:** when the Finnish text is blunt or quiet, keep it quiet in English; avoid emotive padding.
3. **Maintain the multi-voice typography:** chat, headlines, stage directions, logs must look different from narration—don’t homogenise.
4. **One term, one choice:** decide early on *stream* vs *broadcast*; *interview* vs *interrogation*; *mod* vs *moderator*—and apply consistently.
5. **Keep handles exactly:** Reakti0n, NordicTruth88, PaperTrail, CherryKitten—don’t “normalise” spelling.
6. **Numbers are part of the drama:** preserve counters, time-stamps, logs, and the clipped list style (e.g., “05:31 – Footsteps in the stairwell.”).
7. **Swearing calibrated:** match intensity scene-by-scene; avoid US swears and avoid turning every “vittu” into the same English word.
8. **Let Finnish everyday detail stand:** coffee makers, tram noise, discount stickers, fog, harbour concrete—these are not trivia; they’re tone.
9. **No footnote creep:** if you must clarify, do it with a six-word gloss in-line, once.
10. **Keep irony sharp, not camp:** the satire targets platforms, institutions, and moral panic; don’t push it into parody.

### D. 5 common pitfalls to avoid (en-GB localisation)
1. **US-English drift:** “apartment” vs “flat”, “cellphone” vs “mobile”, “attorney” vs “solicitor/lawyer”. Default to UK forms unless context demands neutral.
2. **Over-explaining Finland:** long cultural notes kill pace. Trust the reader; give minimal anchors only.
3. **Flattening the irony:** if you make everything “serious thriller voice”, you lose the media-satire bite; keep the tonal contrasts.
4. **Over-slangification:** too much “innit / fam / bruv” dates instantly and misplaces class/region. Keep slang light and functional.
5. **Inconsistent tech vocabulary:** switching between “stream/broadcast/live”, “clip/snippet”, “mod/moderator” confuses readers and breaks the platform realism.
