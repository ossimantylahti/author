## (1) General principles (en-US)

### Positioning & target feel
- **Genre blend:** literary crime / dark-comedy noir with platform-age satire—“Coen-adjacent” in its deadpan absurdity and moral weirdness, but still *literary* and image-driven. The humor is often **dry, structural, and cruelly situational**, not jokey.
- **Momentum:** keep the narrative **clean and forward-driving**. The book already has strong scene cuts (streams → police → media → private messages). Translation should preserve that kinetic pacing.

### Finland stays Finland (no domestication)
- **All places remain in Finland** (Helsinki districts like Vuosaari, Kallio, Pasila; Bulevardi; Kirkkonummi; Lieksa; Koli; Pielinen; Musiikkitalo; Assembly).
- **No American replacements** for Finnish institutions, stores, or outlets (no “7‑Eleven,” “Walmart,” “NYT,” “FBI” unless the source explicitly mentions a US outlet like Fox News).
- The reading experience should feel like: *“I’m in Helsinki, but I’m reading fluent American English.”*

### Voice & register
- **Narration:** modern literary noir—precise sensory detail, occasional lyrical lift, frequent ironic distance. Keep sentences **tight**; don’t inflate with explanatory clauses.
- **Elias:** grounded, slightly nerdy, self-aware, increasingly hardened. Keep him **plainspoken** with controlled emotion (his big swings land harder if the baseline is restrained).
- **Miksu:** hype-and-hustle producer voice; internet-native. In en-US he can say “bro,” “this is huge,” “content,” “algorithm,” but avoid caricature.
- **Virtanen:** procedural, exhausted, principled but pressured. Use **neutral US police language** (interview room, evidence, custody) while preserving that he’s **Finnish police** (no US-legal boilerplate unless the text calls for it).
- **Arkkitehti:** controlled, aesthetic, quasi-religious rationalism. Use **cold, architectural diction**; aphoristic lines should feel like engineered steel.

### Comedy, irony, and dark satirical bite
- The book’s funniest moments are often:
  - bureaucracy colliding with the internet,
  - tragedy colliding with branding (“sponsor logo in the corner”),
  - Finland’s quiet surfaces hiding grotesque spectacle,
  - “too real” platform reactions.
- Translate to **American deadpan**: understatement, bluntness, and timing. Avoid stand-up style or punchline rewriting.
- **Let the absurdity stand** (e.g., “Plussa card” used during a crime; selfie evidence; “Uno cards” in Seiska). Don’t over-explain the joke.

### Suspense mechanics
- Preserve suspense through:
  - **information gating** (what police know vs what the stream reveals),
  - **process** (evidence handling, chain-of-custody vibe, legal/ethical constraints),
  - **public noise** (tips flooding; media spirals; clip economy).
- Avoid smoothing contradictions too early; the novel uses misreadings (e.g., the beanie text) as tension devices.

### Twitch chat / chorus / system: keep layers distinct
The text uses multiple “channels”:
1. **Twitch chat** (fast, meme-y, often bilingual).
2. **News headlines/outlets** (YLE vs Iltalehti vs HS vs MikroBitti vs Seiska).
3. **Chorus / @system** (tragic chorus meets machine log).
**Rule:** preserve formatting and voice separation:
- handles in brackets,
- short lines,
- emojis only when present,
- technical log strings kept technical.

### Spoken language & swearing (en-US)
- Use natural US profanity, but **don’t oversalt**: the Finnish original’s harshness is often brief and sharp.
- Suggested intensity mapping (context-sensitive):
  - “vittu” → “fuck,” “fucking,” “holy shit”
  - “helvetti” → “hell,” “what the hell,” “goddamn”
  - “perkele” → usually translate by effect (“for fuck’s sake”), or keep **perkele** once as Finnish flavor (explain once) but don’t turn it into a gimmick.
- **Avoid teen slang** and fast-aging TikTok phrases. Prefer durable contemporary US internet language: “WTF,” “no way,” “this is insane,” “clip it,” “cap,” “POG” (sparingly), “IRL,” “true crime.”

### Technical terms & anglicisms
- Streaming/e-sports terms are already English-adjacent: keep them fluent and accurate (stream, VOD, mod, ban, overlay, slow mode, bitrate, LUFS, SHA‑256).
- Don’t “translate tech into prose explanations” unless the source already frames it as explanation. The novel often uses technicality as *texture* and *authority*.

### Explain vs leave untranslated (footnotes discouraged)
- **Footnotes discouraged**: they break thriller pace and multi-voice rhythm.
- Use:
  - **Keep original + explain once** for recurring Finland-specific brands/institutions that matter.
  - **Brief in-sentence gloss** where needed for first-time clarity.
  - **Unobtrusive appositive** (“Alepa, a neighborhood convenience store”).
- Leave unexplained:
  - street names, minor local references that read as atmosphere,
  - most Finnish proper nouns once anchored.

### C. 10 concrete rules for the translator (en-US)
1. **Keep Finland in the text:** don’t substitute Finnish brands/institutions with American equivalents; anchor with minimal gloss once.
2. **Maintain deadpan:** let absurdity land through timing and restraint; don’t add jokes or moral commentary.
3. **Preserve the multi-channel typography:** chat/logs/headlines remain visually distinct; do not normalize into narrative paragraphs.
4. **Choose one tech term and stick to it:** stream (not broadcast), clip (not snippet), mod (not admin), VOD (not recording) unless context demands.
5. **Handles must be exact:** Reakti0n with a zero, NordicTruth88, PaperTrail, CherryKitten; never “correct” them.
6. **Numbers are narrative beats:** keep viewer/subscriber counters, timestamps, hashes, bitrate/LUFS values intact.
7. **Swearing by intensity, not literal:** calibrate profanity to scene; avoid teen slang and avoid “cute” substitutes that deflate tension.
8. **Finnish everyday detail is tone:** coffee, discount stickers, fog, tram squeal, slush, bureaucracy—keep concrete and unromantic.
9. **Under-explain rather than over-explain:** if the context carries it, let it; avoid footnotes and touristy asides.
10. **Don’t Americanize legal culture:** no Miranda copy-paste, no DA-speak; keep generic US English with Finnish institutional flavor.

### D. 5 common mistakes to avoid (en-US)
1. **Euro-English register drift:** overly formal British phrasing (“whilst,” “queue,” “flat”)—stay US standard (“while,” “line,” “apartment/flat depending on context; Helsinki housing can be “apartment” but “studio” often fits).
2. **Over-explaining Finland:** long cultural lectures break pacing; give a small gloss once and move on.
3. **Over-straightening the story:** the novel’s power is contradiction (tragedy + marketing, truth + performance); don’t simplify into clean procedural beats.
4. **Over-dramatizing emotion:** keep the cold, observed style; let shock come from detail and silence, not extra adjectives.
5. **Inconsistent tech vocabulary:** swapping terms (stream/broadcast/live, clip/snippet, mod/moderator) weakens realism and confuses platform-savvy readers.
