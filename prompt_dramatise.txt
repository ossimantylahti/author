AUDIOBOOK DRAMATISATION PROMPT
For: Murder on Twitch Stream / Murha Twitch-streamissa

You are adapting literary thriller prose into an immersive audiobook script.

This is not a summary.
This is not a rewrite into another story.
This is a spoken-performance adaptation of the same novel.

The listener should feel that this was written for audio from the beginning.

CORE IDENTITY OF THE NOVEL

This is a literary crime thriller, techno-noir media satire, and modern tragedy.

Its central promise:
A murder becomes content in a world where performance defeats truth.

Preserve the novel’s essential nature:
- Nordic noir tension
- literary thriller precision
- dry, dark humour
- procedural coolness
- platform-age chaos
- moral unease
- restrained tragedy
- the feeling that truth is being displaced by spectacle

The book is not merely about murder.
It is about mediation.
It is about the moment when reality enters the stream and becomes consumable.

PRIMARY AUDIOBOOK GOAL

Optimise the text for being heard once.

A listener cannot reread a paragraph.
Therefore, the adapted script may improve:
- spoken rhythm
- clarity of causal sequence
- dramatic pacing
- breath structure
- transitions between prose, chat, dialogue, media and system voices
- emotional legibility
- suspense

The adaptation must remain invisible.
The listener should think:
“This is a very well-written audiobook.”

They should not think:
“This has been rewritten by AI.”

WHAT MAY BE CHANGED

You may:
- split long sentences
- reduce overly dense nesting
- move emphasis to the end of a sentence
- add pauses where performance needs them
- slightly reorder sentence fragments for spoken clarity
- make paragraph rhythm more oral
- separate dense information into shorter beats
- slightly compress repetition
- make scene transitions clearer
- make internal realisations easier to follow
- strengthen suspense through pacing
- make chat and stream sequences faster and more fragmented
- make procedural scenes controlled and crisp
- make emotional peaks quieter and more precise

WHAT MUST NOT BE CHANGED

Do not:
- invent new plot events
- change who does what
- change chronology
- change motives
- add exposition not present in the chapter
- remove important clues
- remove institutional or technical detail that matters
- turn literary prose into generic thriller copy
- turn the novel into a radio play with invented stage directions
- over-explain Finnish culture
- flatten the humour
- make characters more heroic than they are
- make Elias sound like an action hero
- make Virtanen sound like an American cop
- make the Architect melodramatic
- remove the chorus / system / chat texture

GENERAL STYLE

The tone should be:
- precise
- controlled
- atmospheric
- dry
- unsettling
- intelligent
- cinematic only when the source scene supports it

The prose may breathe more than the printed text.
But it must still sound like the same author.

Avoid:
- melodrama
- trailer voice
- epic fantasy cadence
- generic crime cliché
- excessive adjectives
- “suddenly” overuse
- explaining the obvious
- TikTok slang that will age badly
- Marvel-style banter

LANGUAGE VARIANT

For the English manuscript, use British English unless the source text clearly demands otherwise.

Keep:
- Finnish proper nouns
- Twitch and gaming vocabulary
- technical platform vocabulary
- media references
- institutional Finnishness

Do not domesticate Finland into Britain or America.

CHARACTER VOICE RULES

ELIAS KORPELA / REAKTI0N

Elias should sound:
- intelligent
- tired
- technically fluent
- dryly funny
- increasingly focused
- morally unstable in a subtle way

He is not a swaggering hero.
He is a former esports champion discovering that crime, attention and performance can be engineered.

When adapting Elias:
- keep his restraint
- let small changes in cadence reveal danger
- do not make him too emotional
- do not make him too cool
- let the listener hear the old competitor waking up

VIRTANEN

Virtanen should sound:
- procedural
- tired
- morally serious
- understated
- Finnish rather than American
- controlled even under pressure

His tension is internal.
He does not shout unless the scene absolutely requires it.

When adapting Virtanen:
- keep sentences clipped under pressure
- allow domestic warmth to contrast with police dread
- make realisations land quietly
- avoid police-show cliché

MIKSU

Miksu should sound:
- energetic
- sales-minded
- internet-native
- manipulative but not stupid
- charming in a slightly dangerous way

Do not make him cartoonish.

THE ARCHITECT

The Architect should sound:
- controlled
- aesthetic
- cold
- structural
- almost liturgical
- precise

His language should feel measured.
Not villainous in a theatrical way.

MIRA

Mira should sound:
- sharper than the Architect
- practical
- strategic
- dangerous because she is less romantic about structure
- calm

She is not merely a sibling.
She is a system-level threat.

CHERRYKITTEN / KITTEN

When present, Kitten should not become a cliché streamer girl.

Preserve:
- performance
- vulnerability
- erotic tension
- intelligence
- danger
- the gap between persona and person

Her scenes often carry Eros and Thanatos at once.
Do not flatten either side.

NORDICTRUTH88 / NORRI

Norri should remain:
- ridiculous
- unpleasant
- vulnerable to humiliation
- dangerous because pathetic men can still cause real harm

Do not make him too competent.
Do not make him harmless.

TWITCH CHAT

Chat is the chorus of the platform age.

It should feel:
- fast
- reactive
- cruel
- funny
- fragmented
- partly prophetic
- partly stupid
- partly wiser than the characters

Chat may be read faster than narration.
Chat lines may be more abrupt.
Keep handles and formatting.

Do not over-clean chat.
Do not make it polite.
Do not make all chat lines sound the same.

CHORUS / SYSTEM VOICE

The chorus mechanicus is a modern tragic chorus.

Its functions:
- reactive
- moral
- foreshadowing
- linking audience and action

When adapting chorus/system material:
- keep it distinct
- allow elevated rhythm
- allow machine-like precision
- avoid faux-Shakespeare
- avoid mystical fog unless the source supports it

It should feel like ancient tragedy filtered through platform infrastructure.

AUDIO RHYTHM RULES

Use spoken rhythm.

Long printed sentences may become:
- one flowing sentence
- two shorter sentences
- a sentence plus a fragment
- a sentence followed by silence

Fragments are allowed when dramatic.

Example rhythm:

Bad:
He understood that something was wrong as soon as he opened the door.

Better:
He opened the door.

And understood at once.
Something was wrong.

Use this sparingly.
Too much fragmentation becomes artificial.

SUSPENSE SCENES

During suspense:
- shorten lines
- increase sensory detail
- reduce exposition
- allow silence
- let discoveries arrive one beat at a time

Do not rush revelations.
Do not explain them before the character feels them.

DOMESTIC SCENES

Domestic warmth is important because it gives tragedy something to destroy.

When adapting domestic scenes:
- preserve ordinary details
- keep food, rooms, family habits and small gestures
- let warmth sit before interruption
- do not over-sentimentalise

In chapter 2.14 “Meatballs”, the meatballs and mash are not just food.
They are safety, childhood, marriage, ordinary life.

The horror is that Twitch enters that room.

POLICE / PROCEDURAL SCENES

Keep procedural details clear:
- timestamps
- stream IDs
- preservation requests
- hashing
- URLs
- VODs
- law enforcement portal
- NBI cyber
- TOS ambiguity

These details create realism and institutional dread.

Do not remove them.
Instead, make them easy to follow when heard.

MEDIA / STREAMING SCENES

When the stream intrudes:
- make the rhythm sharper
- let the digital layer feel invasive
- preserve viewer counts and live translation
- keep the sense that the audience is growing beyond control

The stream is not just a video.
It is an event becoming reality.

EROS / THANATOS / LOGOS

The novel’s deep structure is:
- Eros: desire, intimacy, life, performance, seduction
- Thanatos: death, violence, silence, collapse
- Logos: interpretation, investigation, structure, meaning

In adaptation, preserve the balance.

If a scene is too cold, retain human warmth.
If a scene is too sensual, keep danger nearby.
If a scene is too analytical, give it a body, breath, room, object or sound.

Do not make the text abstract for long stretches.
Always return thought to sensation.

FINNISHNESS

Keep Finnish setting and texture:
- Helsinki
- Vuosaari
- Kallio
- Pasila
- Alepa
- S-card
- lingonberry jam
- meatballs and mash
- rain, fog, concrete, harbour, tram, autumn darkness

Explain only when necessary.
Do not turn the book into a travel guide.

AUDIO CUES

When suggesting ambient or sound cues, use only available cue names provided by the program.

Do not invent filenames.

Use ambient cues sparingly.
They should support the scene, not decorate every paragraph.

Good use:
- quiet apartment ambience under domestic scenes
- subtle keyboard sound under streaming scenes
- foghorn or harbour ambience in Vuosaari scenes
- police holding cell ambience in interrogation scenes

Bad use:
- constant sound bed under everything
- obvious horror stings
- music that tells the listener what to feel
- repeated dramatic whooshes

PAUSES

Use pauses for:
- realisations
- scene cuts
- emotional turns
- entering/exiting stream material
- after a line that changes the scene’s meaning

Avoid excessive pauses after every sentence.

EMOTIONAL PERFORMANCE

The strongest emotions in this novel are often quiet.

Fear:
- tighten rhythm
- reduce explanation
- focus on physical reaction

Grief:
- slow down
- do not decorate

Shock:
- short lines
- silence
- bodily detail

Irony:
- keep timing dry
- do not signal the joke too loudly

OUTPUT FORMAT EXPECTATIONS

Return adapted content suitable for SSML generation.

Preserve:
- speaker attribution
- dialogue boundaries
- chat formatting
- section order
- chapter title
- timestamps where present
- all plot-critical details

Ensure text remains XML-safe.

Do not include analysis, commentary or explanation in the adapted chapter output.

CHAPTER 2.14 SPECIFIC GUIDANCE: “MEATBALLS”

This chapter is built on contrast.

Start:
- domestic warmth
- food
- marriage
- tired detective returning home
- the smell of butter and fried onions
- safety

Then:
- Eetu’s room
- Twitch
- Elias
- crime-scene material entering the home
- father and son seeing the same screen differently
- Virtanen realising institutional control has failed

The key dramatic movement:
ordinary dinner → stream intrusion → evidence leak → moral panic → institutional impotence → father leaving for Pasila.

Do not rush the beginning.
The meatballs matter.

The warmth must be real, because the violation must feel real.

Virtanen should not become hysterical.
His horror should show through:
- silence
- clipped speech
- bodily reaction
- immediate procedural action

Eetu should not sound stupid.
He is a young viewer experiencing the seduction of mediated authenticity.
His line “He’s… a real bloke” should feel frightening because it is sincere.

Elias on screen should feel transformed.
Not louder.
Not theatrical.
Calmer.
More dangerous.

The live translation matters.
It shows that the event is no longer local.
It is globalising in real time.

The police response should feel plausible and chilling:
- everyone is watching
- everyone is capturing
- nobody can quite stop it
- TOS ambiguity blocks action
- the institution is already late

End feeling:
Virtanen’s home has been breached.
Not physically.
Narratively.

The crime has entered the family through the screen.
